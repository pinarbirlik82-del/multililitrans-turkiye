Literary Multilingualism in Türkiye

Festivals, translation practices and cultural encounters: exploring how languages meet, circulate and become visible in literary public life.

COST Action CA24137 Pilot country page · Türkiye Discussion draft
01 / Introduction

Many languages.
Connected literary worlds.

Türkiye offers a rich field for examining literary multilingualism — shaped by migration, regional language histories, translation networks, urban cultural encounters and diverse forms of literary participation.

This contribution approaches festivals and literary gatherings as public spaces in which languages, authors, readers, publishers and cultural institutions encounter one another. It asks not only which languages are present, but also which are translated, heard, represented, or left at the margins.

Research perspective — from events to practices

The focus is not a catalogue of festivals. Events are treated as research sites through which we can investigate language visibility, translation, cultural mediation, participation and access.

The interpretations on this page are analytical starting points. They are not claims that every festival explicitly identifies itself as multilingual. Further interviews and programme-level analysis are needed.

02 / Shared analytical framework

What should we look for?

A common set of questions can make national contributions comparable without flattening their local contexts.

01 · Policy

Institutional and language policy

How do organisers, municipalities, universities and cultural institutions frame linguistic diversity? Are there explicit policies on language representation, translation or minority-language participation?

02 · Practice

Literary and linguistic practices

Which languages appear in readings, panels, publications and workshops? Are multilingual writing, code-switching, oral traditions or translation discussed and practised?

03 · Participation

Who participates?

Which authors, translators, publishers, readers and communities take part? Who speaks, who listens, and who shapes the programme?

04 · Accessibility

Access and inclusion

Are events free and open? Is interpretation available? Are venues, formats and communication accessible to different audiences?

05 · Translation

Translation and circulation

Which works travel between languages? Are translators visible as cultural agents? Do professional meetings create pathways for literary exchange?

06 · Intercultural dialogue

Encounter and negotiation

How do events create dialogue across linguistic, regional, national and cultural boundaries? Are differences explored or merely celebrated?

07 · Sustainability

Continuity and legacy

Are programmes, recordings, translations and archives preserved? What remains available after the event, and who maintains it?

03 / Türkiye · Selected cases

Literary spaces across the country

The following cases illustrate different institutional forms and possible research angles. They are a selected sample, not an exhaustive inventory.

Istanbul
International festival
01

İTEF — Istanbul International Literature Festival

Organised since 2009 by Kalem Culture Association, İTEF brings local and international authors, publishers and literary professionals into contact with Turkish literature and the publishing field. Its Professional Meetings / Fellowship programme connects authors, publishers, agents, translators and journalists.

Why it matters for MultiLiLiTrans

İTEF offers a strong case for studying translation as cultural mediation and the international circulation of literature. Its professional network makes it possible to ask how works move from Turkish into other languages, how translators and publishers select texts, and which literary voices gain international visibility.

Evidence to collect: fellowship participants, language pairs, translated titles, programme languages, interpretation practices.

Bursa / Nilüfer
International festival
02

Bursa International Literature Festival (BUEF)

BUEF presents literature as a meeting ground for different voices and ideas. Its 2026 programme is announced for 26–29 November, with writers from Türkiye and several other countries. The published programme information also identifies international cultural and literary partners.

Why it matters for MultiLiLiTrans

BUEF can be examined as a site where international literary exchange is organised through themes, invited authors and institutional partnerships. The key question is how the presence of writers from different countries translates into actual multilingual interaction: readings, discussion languages, interpretation and translated texts.

Istanbul
Public literary gathering
03

International Sarıyer Literature Days

Organised by Sarıyer Municipality since 2012, the event combines author talks, book signings, workshops, exhibitions, concerts and activities for younger audiences. Municipal reporting on the 2025 edition records a five-day programme with 400 writers and 33 publishers; the 2026 edition was also presented as a large public literary gathering.

Why it matters for MultiLiLiTrans

Sarıyer offers a case for examining literature in public space and the relationship between scale, participation and linguistic diversity. A large audience does not automatically mean multilingual access; the programme needs to be examined for language choice, interpretation, minority-language visibility and the presence of translated literature.

Participation Public space Accessibility

Diyarbakır
Regional book fair
04

Diyarbakır Book Fair

Organised by TÜYAP in cooperation with the Turkish Publishers Association, the fair brings publishers, civil-society organisations, authors and readers together in the region. The 2026 edition included a programme of talks, signing sessions, workshops, theatre, music and poetry events; the organiser describes the fair as contributing to the social and cultural life of Diyarbakır and its surroundings.

Why it matters for MultiLiLiTrans

Diyarbakır is a particularly relevant site for investigating the relationship between regional literary cultures, language visibility and publishing. Research should establish which languages and literary traditions are represented, whether Kurdish-language publishing and authors are present, and how audiences encounter multilingual materials. These questions require programme and participant verification rather than assumption.

The four cases deliberately represent different models: an international literary network, a themed international festival, a municipality-led public literary gathering and a regional publishing fair. This variety can help the working group compare not only events, but also the infrastructures through which literary multilingualism becomes visible.

04 / Emerging questions

What might the Türkiye case reveal?

Visibility is not the same as presence

A language may be spoken by participants or present in a city's social life without appearing in the official programme. The research should distinguish between languages used in everyday contexts, languages represented on stage, languages available in publications and languages supported through interpretation.

Translation is infrastructure

Translation is not simply a final step in literary circulation. It is shaped by publishers, agents, translators, funding, institutional partnerships and selection practices. İTEF's professional meetings provide one route for investigating these networks.

Access needs to be examined in practice

Free admission, open-air venues and large audiences can widen participation, but they do not by themselves guarantee linguistic accessibility. Interpretation, multilingual publicity, accessible archives and the affordability of books are also relevant.

Comparative prompts

  1. Which languages are named in festival policies and programmes?
  2. Who decides which authors and languages are represented?
  3. Are translators visible, credited and invited to speak?
  4. Are minority and regional languages represented as literary languages?
  5. What forms of interpretation are offered, and for whom?
  6. Do digital archives preserve multilingual content?
  7. What barriers do organisers and participants identify?

06 / A living exhibition

A shared platform, not a static catalogue

The Türkiye example can serve as a pilot structure for the other participating countries. Each national team can contribute a small number of well-documented cases using the same template, while adding local themes and materials.

Translation Minority-language visibility Literary festivals Migration Publishing networks Public participation

The platform could connect country pages through shared themes, allowing visitors to move between national contexts and identify common patterns as well as meaningful differences.

A sustainable first version does not need complex functionality. A clear country-page structure, accessible text, carefully selected media, stable links and a maintainable archive may be more valuable than an expensive feature-rich website.
